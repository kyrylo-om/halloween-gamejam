The Causes of the French Revolution

The French Revolution began in 1789 and changed Europe forever. Its causes were political, economic and social. The monarchy of Louis XVI was weak and could not solve the problems of the country. France was deeply in debt after expensive wars, including its support of the American Revolution. Bad harvests made bread too expensive for ordinary people, and many of them were hungry.

Society was divided into three estates. The clergy and the nobility paid almost no taxes, while the Third Estate carried the whole burden. At the same time, the ideas of the Enlightenment taught people that power should come from the people and not from the king.

When the Estates-General met in 1789, the Third Estate declared itself the National Assembly. A few weeks later, the people of Paris stormed the Bastille, and the old order began to fall.
